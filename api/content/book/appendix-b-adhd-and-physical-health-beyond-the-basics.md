# Appendix B: ADHD and Physical Health — Beyond the Basics

The connection between ADHD and physical health extends well beyond the sleep, nutrition, and exercise foundation covered in Chapter 5. Adults with ADHD face distinctive health challenges that are direct consequences of the executive function deficits that define the condition: difficulty scheduling and attending medical appointments, inconsistent medication adherence, higher rates of accidental injury from impulsivity, challenges with chronic health management, and the physical consequences of chronic stress from ADHD-related life difficulties.

## Medical Care and ADHD

Managing healthcare with ADHD is a genuine challenge that primary care providers rarely address explicitly. Scheduling appointments requires the long-horizon planning that ADHD impairs. Following through on referrals requires sustained, sequential action over days or weeks. Completing intake paperwork requires sustained attention to detail. Remembering to take prescribed medications at consistent times requires working memory reliability. And returning for follow-up appointments requires the kind of future-oriented behavior that time blindness consistently undermines.

The solution is systems, not willpower. A healthcare management system for ADHD includes: a single digital health folder containing all insurance cards, medication lists, and appointment records; medication stored in highly visible locations with visual reminders (a pill organizer in the bathroom, a timer that goes off at medication time); appointment booking done immediately when the need is identified (never 'later'); and a trusted person — a partner, an adult child, or a close friend — who can serve as an accountability partner for healthcare follow-through.

## Chronic Conditions and ADHD

Managing chronic health conditions — diabetes, hypertension, thyroid disorders, autoimmune conditions — with ADHD adds a layer of executive function demand that can overwhelm already-stretched systems. The monitoring requirements, medication adherence schedules, dietary restrictions, and lifestyle management protocols of chronic conditions all require exactly the sustained, consistent, detail-oriented self-regulation that ADHD impairs. Research suggests that adults with ADHD have significantly worse outcomes for some chronic conditions due to difficulty adhering to management protocols.

The chronic condition management strategy for ADHD follows the same automation principle as financial management: remove as much human decision-making as possible from the management protocol. Automated medication dispensers. Pre-set glucose monitor alerts. Meal planning services for dietary-restricted eating. Telemedicine for follow-up appointments that can happen in ten minutes without travel. And, critically, direct communication with healthcare providers about ADHD as a factor in treatment adherence, so that the treatment plan can be designed with realistic assumptions about what the patient can consistently execute.

## Injury, Impulsivity, and Physical Risk

Adults with ADHD have significantly higher rates of accidental injury than neurotypical peers, a direct consequence of impulsivity and the attentional lapses that occur when the ADHD brain shifts between tasks or becomes bored with routine safety precautions. Car accidents occur at dramatically elevated rates in adults with ADHD, a finding that Barkley and colleagues have documented extensively. Sports and recreational injuries, kitchen accidents, and workplace injuries are similarly elevated.

Risk awareness for ADHD is not about cultivating fear or eliminating the risk-taking that is both a liability and an asset of ADHD. It is about building deliberate routines around high-risk activities: always using a seatbelt as a non-negotiable habit rather than a conscious decision, following a pre-activity checklist for potentially dangerous physical activities, and recognizing the elevated injury risk during states of emotional dysregulation or fatigue.

## KEY INSIGHT

Physical health management is an ADHD challenge, not a general wellness topic. The executive function demands of scheduling, adhering, monitoring, and following through are genuine barriers for the ADHD brain. Systems that automate and simplify healthcare management are not conveniences — they are clinical necessities.
