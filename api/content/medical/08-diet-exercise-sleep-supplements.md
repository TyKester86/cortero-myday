# Diet, exercise, sleep and supplements: what the evidence says (and doesn't)

### The baseline: eat well, move, sleep
summary: Guidelines stress a balanced diet, good nutrition and regular exercise for children, young people and adults with ADHD. These support health and wellbeing; they are not a replacement for treatment.
source: NICE — NG87, recommendation 1.6.1 (last updated 13 September 2019)
url: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87

### Sugar
summary: Well-controlled studies found sugar does not change children's behavior or thinking; a small effect in some children can't be completely ruled out. There is no evidence sugar causes ADHD.
source: Wolraich ML et al. — The effect of sugar on behavior or cognition in children: a meta-analysis. JAMA 274(20):1617–1621 (November 1995)
url: https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.1995.03530200053037

### Food dyes and elimination ("few foods") diets — evidence is mixed and limited
summary: A meta-analysis found restriction diets reduced symptoms in some children (a small-to-moderate effect), and estimated about 8% of children with ADHD may have symptoms related to synthetic food colors — but the food-color effects were small and vulnerable to publication bias. When raters didn't know who was on the diet, effects mostly faded except for artificial-color exclusion in children already selected for food sensitivities. A strictly supervised elimination diet can help a specialist test whether foods affect one child's behavior; diets based on IgG blood tests should be discouraged. NICE advises against cutting out colourings and additives as a general treatment, suggests keeping a food-and-behavior diary when parents notice a link, and says there's no evidence about long-term benefits or harms of "few food" diets. Any restrictive diet for a child should involve a doctor or dietitian.
source: Nigg JT et al. — Meta-analysis of ADHD symptoms, restriction diet, and synthetic food color additives. J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 51(1):86–97 (January 2012); Sonuga-Barke EJ et al. — Am J Psychiatry 170(3):275–289 (March 2013); Pelsser LM et al. — INCA study, Lancet 377(9764):494–503 (February 2011); NICE NG87 1.6.2–1.6.5 (last updated 13 September 2019)
url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2011.10.015

### Omega-3 / fish oil — evidence says it doesn't help core symptoms
summary: The newest Cochrane review (37 trials, over 2,300 children and teens) found high-certainty evidence that omega-3 and other fatty-acid supplements had no effect on parent-rated ADHD symptoms, inattention or hyperactivity compared with placebo; side effects were similar to placebo. An older meta-analysis had found a small effect. NICE says not to offer fatty-acid supplements to treat ADHD in children and young people.
source: Gillies D et al. — Polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) for ADHD in children and adolescents. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2023(4):CD007986 (April 2023); NICE NG87 1.6.4 (last updated 13 September 2019)
url: https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007986.pub3

### Exercise — promising, but the evidence is small
summary: A meta-analysis of 8 small randomized trials (249 children) found short-term aerobic exercise had moderate-to-large effects on attention, hyperactivity, impulsivity, anxiety and executive function, and yoga suggested improvement too. The studies were few, small and short, so this is encouraging but not strong evidence; exercise is a healthy add-on, not a replacement for treatment.
source: Cerrillo-Urbina AJ et al. — The effects of physical exercise in children with ADHD: a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized control trials. Child Care Health Dev 41(6):779–788 (November 2015)
url: https://doi.org/10.1111/cch.12255

### Melatonin for trouble falling asleep
summary: In a randomized, placebo-controlled trial in 105 children aged 6–12 with ADHD and long-standing trouble falling asleep, melatonin for 4 weeks helped them fall asleep about 27 minutes earlier and sleep about 20 minutes longer, with no serious side effects — but it did not change behavior, thinking or quality of life. Melatonin addresses sleep timing, not ADHD itself. Talk with your child's doctor before starting it.
source: Van der Heijden KB et al. — Effect of melatonin on sleep, behavior, and cognition in ADHD and chronic sleep-onset insomnia. J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 46(2):233–241 (February 2007)
url: https://doi.org/10.1097/01.chi.0000246055.76167.0d

### Where evidence is missing
summary: In a large review of non-medication treatments, cognitive ("brain") training, neurofeedback and elimination diets looked helpful only when rated by people who knew who got them; with blinded raters the effects were no longer significant, so the evidence that they treat core ADHD symptoms is weak. For anything this reference doesn't cover (other supplements, products marketed for focus, newer trends), Hana says the evidence isn't established in her library rather than guessing.
source: Sonuga-Barke EJ et al. — Nonpharmacological interventions for ADHD: systematic review and meta-analyses. Am J Psychiatry 170(3):275–289 (March 2013); Faraone SV et al. — World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement (September 2021)
url: https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991
