# ADHD in adults vs. kids

### How ADHD changes with age
summary: ADHD starts in childhood, and symptoms can change as people grow. Hyperactivity may fade or show up as extreme inner restlessness in adults. Symptoms can feel worse when adult responsibilities grow, causing trouble at work, at home and in relationships. Teens and adults with ADHD are more likely to take risks such as substance use and unsafe sex, and are at higher risk of job and money problems.
source: CDC — ADHD in adults (last updated June 1, 2026); National Institute of Mental Health — ADHD (revised August 2026)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/articles/adhd-across-the-lifetime.html

### How many adults have ADHD (United States)
summary: A 2023 national survey estimated 15.5 million U.S. adults (6.0%) had a current ADHD diagnosis, and about half were diagnosed at 18 or older. About a third had taken a stimulant in the past year, and most of those (71.5%) had trouble getting a prescription filled because of shortages. About half had used telehealth for ADHD care.
source: Staley BS et al., CDC National Center for Health Statistics — ADHD diagnosis, treatment, and telehealth use in adults. MMWR 73(40):890–895 (October 2024)
url: https://doi.org/10.15585/mmwr.mm7340a1

### Does ADHD go away?
summary: For many people it doesn't fully. An international consensus statement reports that only about one in six young people with ADHD still meet the full criteria at age 25, but about half still have signs of the condition that affect their lives. European experts describe ADHD as often a lifelong, impairing condition that is underdiagnosed and undertreated in adults, and call for more research on differences between men and women and on older adults.
source: Faraone SV et al. — World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 128:789–818 (September 2021); Kooij JJS et al. — Updated European Consensus Statement on diagnosis and treatment of adult ADHD. Eur Psychiatry 56:14–34 (February 2019)
url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eurpsy.2018.11.001

### Treatment for adults
summary: ADHD in adults can often be managed with the right treatment, usually medication (mainly stimulants) together with therapy and practical behavioral strategies, adjusted over time. UK guidance offers medication to adults whose symptoms still cause significant problems after changes to their environment have been tried, with lisdexamfetamine or methylphenidate first-line.
source: CDC — ADHD in adults (last updated June 1, 2026); NICE — NG87 (last updated 13 September 2019)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/articles/adhd-across-the-lifetime.html
