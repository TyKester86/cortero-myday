# ADHD in women and girls

### Why girls and women are missed
summary: Boys are diagnosed far more often than girls (in the U.S., 13% vs 7% of children). An expert consensus says this gap is at least partly because girls are not recognized or referred. Girls and women often have a more subtle, inward presentation — more inattention and internal restlessness than disruptive behavior — and coping strategies that mask symptoms, plus anxiety or depression that can overshadow the ADHD underneath. The experts urge clinicians to stop thinking of ADHD as only a "behavior problem" and to watch for the internalized presentation common in females.
source: Young S et al. — Females with ADHD: an expert consensus statement taking a lifespan approach. BMC Psychiatry 20(1):404 (August 2020); CDC — Data and Statistics on ADHD (last updated July 8, 2026)
url: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-02707-9

### Across a woman's life
summary: The same consensus recommends a lifespan approach to care for women with ADHD, because their symptoms and needs change through major transitions alongside changes in their social circumstances, and says treatment with medication and psychological support is expected to improve long-term outcomes. European experts list differences between men and women with ADHD as an area that still needs more research.
source: Young S et al. — Females with ADHD: an expert consensus statement. BMC Psychiatry 20(1):404 (August 2020); Kooij JJS et al. — Updated European Consensus Statement on adult ADHD. Eur Psychiatry 56:14–34 (February 2019)
url: https://doi.org/10.1186/s12888-020-02707-9
