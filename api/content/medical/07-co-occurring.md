# Conditions that often come with ADHD

### It's usually not just ADHD
summary: In a national parent survey, nearly 78% of children with ADHD had at least one other condition: almost half had a behavior or conduct problem and about 4 in 10 had anxiety; depression, autism and Tourette syndrome also occur. NIMH notes that ADHD often comes with disruptive behavior, learning disorders, sleep problems, anxiety or depression, which can make each harder to diagnose and treat. That's why guidelines tell clinicians to screen for these.
source: CDC — Data and Statistics on ADHD (last updated July 8, 2026); National Institute of Mental Health — ADHD (revised August 2026)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/data/index.html

### The wider list (all ages)
summary: An international consensus lists the conditions that commonly occur with ADHD: depression, bipolar disorder, autism, anxiety disorders, oppositional defiant disorder, conduct disorder, eating disorders and substance use disorders.
source: Faraone SV et al. — World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 128:789–818 (September 2021)
url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022

### Sleep problems
summary: Sleep problems are common in children with ADHD. Expert recommendations put healthy sleep habits first (a regular schedule and calm routine), with behavioral approaches as the first treatment for insomnia — though more trials are needed to prove they work in ADHD specifically. Other causes such as sleep apnea, restless legs, other conditions or ADHD medicine itself should be checked. For trouble falling asleep, randomized trials support melatonin; evidence for other sleep medicines is more limited. Any sleep medicine for a child should be discussed with their doctor.
source: Cortese S et al. — Assessment and management of sleep problems in youths with ADHD. J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry 52(8):784–796 (August 2013)
url: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2013.06.001
