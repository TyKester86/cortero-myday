# Behavioral strategies, therapy and school support

### Parent training comes first for young children
summary: For children under 6, experts recommend parent training in behavior management before medication. In the UK, NICE recommends an ADHD-focused group parent-training programme as the first treatment for parents of children under 5.
source: CDC — Treatment of ADHD (last updated June 2, 2026); NICE — NG87 (last updated 13 September 2019)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/index.html

### Classroom and school supports
summary: Behavioral classroom interventions and peer interventions focused on behavior help, and different treatments are often most effective used together. The American Academy of Pediatrics says school-age children and teens with ADHD should have educational supports, which can include an IEP or a 504 plan.
source: CDC — Treatment of ADHD (last updated June 2, 2026); Wolraich ML et al., American Academy of Pediatrics — Clinical Practice Guideline, Pediatrics 144(4):e20192528 (October 2019)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/treatment/index.html

### Therapy for teens and adults
summary: NICE suggests a course of CBT (cognitive behavioural therapy) for young people who have benefited from medication but are still significantly impaired, focusing on social skills, problem-solving, self-control, active listening and handling feelings. For adults, it suggests non-medication treatment for those who choose not to take medicine, have trouble sticking with it, or haven't been helped enough by it. NIMH lists psychosocial treatments such as CBT, parent training and school interventions alongside medication.
source: NICE — NG87 (last updated 13 September 2019); National Institute of Mental Health — ADHD (revised August 2026)
url: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87

### How strong the evidence is for non-medication treatments
summary: Experts agree non-medication treatments are less effective than medication for the core symptoms but are often useful for problems that remain once medication is optimized. A large meta-analysis found that behavioral therapy, neurofeedback, cognitive (brain) training and elimination diets looked helpful when rated by people who knew who got the treatment, but the effects shrank to non-significant when raters were blinded — so for core ADHD symptoms the evidence for these is still weak. They can still help with problems that remain once medication is working.
source: Faraone SV et al. — World Federation of ADHD International Consensus Statement, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 128:789–818 (September 2021); Sonuga-Barke EJ et al. — Nonpharmacological interventions for ADHD: systematic review and meta-analyses. Am J Psychiatry 170(3):275–289 (March 2013)
url: https://doi.org/10.1176/appi.ajp.2012.12070991
