# How ADHD is diagnosed

### The diagnostic criteria (DSM-5-TR)
summary: Clinicians use the criteria in the DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association, 2022). Children up to age 16 need six or more symptoms of inattention and/or six or more of hyperactivity-impulsivity; people 17 and older (including adults) need five or more. Symptoms must have lasted at least 6 months; several must have been present before age 12; several must show up in two or more settings (such as home, school or work, with friends or relatives); there must be clear evidence they interfere with or reduce the quality of social, school or work life; and they must not be better explained by another mental disorder.
source: CDC — Clinical Care of ADHD (for healthcare providers), citing the DSM-5-TR (last updated July 30, 2026)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/hcp/clinical-care/index.html

### There is no single test
summary: No blood test, brain scan or single questionnaire can diagnose ADHD. Diagnosis takes several steps, including gathering information from parents, teachers and other adults who know the child about behavior in different settings — at home, at school and with friends.
source: CDC — Diagnosing ADHD (last updated July 7, 2026)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/diagnosis/index.html

### What pediatricians are told to do
summary: The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends that a child's primary care clinician evaluate any child or teen aged 4 to 18 who has academic or behavior problems plus inattention, hyperactivity or impulsivity; confirm the DSM-5 criteria with reports from parents or guardians, teachers and other school staff, documenting problems in more than one major setting; rule out other causes; and screen for conditions that often come along with ADHD (anxiety, depression, oppositional or conduct problems, learning and language disorders, autism, tics, sleep apnea). ADHD should be managed as a long-term (chronic) condition.
source: Wolraich ML et al., American Academy of Pediatrics — Clinical Practice Guideline for the Diagnosis, Evaluation, and Treatment of ADHD in Children and Adolescents. Pediatrics 144(4):e20192528 (October 2019)
url: https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2019-2528

### Who can diagnose (UK guidance)
summary: In the UK, NICE says ADHD should only be diagnosed by a specialist psychiatrist, paediatrician or other appropriately qualified professional with training in ADHD, based on a full clinical and psychosocial assessment that covers behavior and symptoms across the different settings of the person's everyday life and their developmental history. Symptoms must cause at least moderate impairment.
source: NICE — Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder: diagnosis and management (NG87), published 14 March 2018, last updated 13 September 2019
url: https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng87

### Getting diagnosed as an adult
summary: Many adults are diagnosed later in life even though symptoms started in childhood. An adult assessment usually includes symptom rating checklists and a history of behavior, a check that symptoms appeared before age 12 (sometimes with input from family or friends), and medical and psychological exams to rule out other causes such as anxiety, depression or sleep problems.
source: CDC — ADHD in adults (last updated June 1, 2026)
url: https://www.cdc.gov/adhd/articles/adhd-across-the-lifetime.html
